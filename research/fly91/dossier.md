## 0. Five findings that matter most

1. **The big jets are bleeding, but not losing share.** IndiGo has posted a net loss in **4 of the last 5 quarters**, a net **−₹6,686 cr** across the five (D). Its share is still *rising*: **65.4% in Sept 2026**. Air India group lost **₹22,238 cr in FY26**. ICRA projects an industry loss of **₹36,000–38,000 cr for FY27** and has a negative outlook. **What's new:** in Q2 FY27, IndiGo lost ₹724 cr *even after excluding forex*. A year earlier the loss was almost all currency. Fuel is now the problem.
2. **Fares are up about a third and flyers are dropping off.** IndiGo's yield was **+34% YoY** in Q2 FY27. Domestic traffic fell **6.5% YoY in Sept 2026**, and Jan–Sep 2026 is **down 0.8%**: the year-to-date total has turned negative. ICRA cut its FY27 domestic growth forecast to **3–6%**.
3. **This is the turboprop's moment.** On 3 Sept 2026 FLY91 placed a firm order for **40 ATR 72-600s** (about $1bn at list prices), growing from **6 aircraft to about 60 by 2032**. Chacko says his fuel-to-revenue ratio is **20–22% vs 38–40% for jet operators** (B). IndiGo's own Q2 FY27 numbers are consistent with that: fuel cost is about **42% of revenue per seat-km** (D).
4. **Regional aviation in India has a graveyard.** Over **20 airlines have shut since 1994**. CAG found **52%** of awarded UDAN routes (rounds 1–3) never started. Only **348 of 679** UDAN routes ever operationalised are still flying. The failures share three causes: scale, lessors and distribution.
5. **FLY91 has already built digital foundations.** It has no check-in, no call centre, UPI on board, SmartBag91, Smart Ticket, website convenience-fee waivers, and a MakeMyTrip holiday-package partnership. The obvious headroom is in ancillaries: IndiGo earns about **10.6%** of revenue from them (D), the global average is about **15%**, Ryanair about **32%**, and US ultra-low-cost carriers about **60%**.

**Derived (D):** ancillary is **10.6%** of revenue from operations, about **₹900 per passenger**. Fuel is **42%** of revenue per seat-km (2.44 ÷ 5.76). That independently supports Chacko's "38–40% for jet operators".

**A defensible reading of "IndiGo posted losses":** IndiGo is not losing the market. Its share rose to 65.4% in Sept 2026 and peaked at 67.4% in July. What changed is that the cost of flying jumped and IndiGo is passing it on: fares up about a third, capacity flat to down. That squeezes the most price-sensitive flyers, who are largely the regional and first-time segment. Avoid saying "IndiGo is weakening" in the artefact; it isn't defensible.

## 10. Candidate storyline for the artefact (for your review, not final)

Working title: ***"The turboprop dividend: why FLY91's next growth engine is a storefront, not a seat."*** Each panel uses only A, B or D data.

1. **The moment.** Big-jet economics broke in 2026: IndiGo's losses (D5) even as its share rose; Air India's ₹22k cr; ICRA's ₹36–38k cr; fares +34%; traffic −6.5%.

3. **The graveyard.** 20+ airlines shut. 52% of UDAN routes never started. Only 51% still flying. Regional airlines die of scale and distribution.
4. **The 10× problem.** Six aircraft becoming about 60 means about 1M passengers a year becoming about 10M (D3). India flies 0.1 trips per person. Customer acquisition has to be cheap, digital and in local languages.
5. **What FLY91 already built.** No check-in, no call centre, UPI, SmartBag91, Smart Ticket, fee waivers, MMT packages: the foundations exist.
6. **The headroom.** Ancillaries at 10.6% (IndiGo) → 15% (global) → 32% (Ryanair) → 60% (US ULCCs), plus an adjustable calculator (D13). The reader sets the assumptions; we don't claim FLY91's numbers.
